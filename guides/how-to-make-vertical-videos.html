Video editing guide

How to Make Vertical Videos

Vertical video is designed for a phone held upright. A 9:16 project gives your footage a tall frame, but good results also depend on framing, readable text and careful previewing.

1. Plan for a 9:16 frame

Decide what the viewer should notice first. Keep faces, products and other important subjects near the center of the composition, leaving room around the edges for platform interface elements.

2. Set the project aspect ratio

Open GOPISUNA VIDEO STUDIO and choose the vertical 9:16 project format from the aspect-ratio controls. Check the preview canvas before arranging all your clips.

3. Import and arrange your footage

Import the video, image and audio files you need. Place the main shots in sequence on the visual timeline, then add supporting audio to an audio track if required.

4. Check crop and framing

Preview each shot in the vertical canvas. If the subject is cut off, adjust the clip framing using the editor controls available for that clip. Do not assume a landscape shot will automatically look good when cropped vertically.

5. Keep the edit focused

Short mobile videos benefit from a clear opening, one main idea and a purposeful ending. Remove pauses that do not add meaning and make sure each cut supports the pace of the story.

6. Review audio and export

Listen for distracting background noise and check that the audio begins and ends where expected. Use the Export controls and choose an available output option supported by your browser and device.

Before publishing

Watch the exported video on a phone-sized screen. Confirm that important details are visible, text is readable and no key subject is hidden by the crop.
