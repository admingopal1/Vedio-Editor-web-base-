How to Add Subtitles to a Video for Free

Subtitles make videos watchable with the sound off, which is how most people browse on phones. They also help viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing, and people watching in a second language.

Option 1: Auto captions

Import your clip, open the Subtitles panel and press Auto captions (AI). The speech model downloads the first time (about 40 MB) and then runs in your browser, so your audio is not uploaded. Each phrase becomes its own text clip on a Subs track.

Option 2: Import an SRT or VTT file

If you already have a subtitle file, choose it in the same panel or paste the text. Each cue becomes an editable clip placed at its original time. Tick the start-at-playhead option to shift the whole set.

Fix mistakes

Automatic captions are never perfect, especially with names, accents and background noise. Click a subtitle clip and correct the text, then nudge the start or end on the timeline.

Style for readability

Use the restyle option to set one size, colour and background for every subtitle. Keep lines short, place them in the lower third for landscape video, and keep them clear of the app buttons in vertical video.

Export

Export the project as MP4 to burn the subtitles into the picture, or export the subtitles as an SRT file to upload alongside the video on platforms that support captions.
